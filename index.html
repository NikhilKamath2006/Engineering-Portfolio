PROJECT 01 — CIRCUIT DESIGN

UARC Power Electronics & Pyrotechnics Board

TEAM — Power & Pyrotechnics
CLUB — University of Auckland Rocketry Club
TOOLS — Altium, LTspice

FIG. 1.1 — Final PCB.

FIG. 1.2 — Schematic of the system.

Designed a MOSFET-based pyro enable circuit using an AND gate and NPN transistor so ignition can only trigger when two independent conditions are met.

  • Simulated the circuit in LTspice to check switching behaviour and confirm component choices before building anything.
  • Used datasheets to find optimal specifications for traces and designed the PCB for the power segment of the circuit.
CONSIDERATIONS
  • The circuit was designed so that the launch can only happen after 2 signals are recieved and are stable for 120ms.
  • When laying out the board, the power segment was important, used datasheet to find optimal buck convertor and power plane placement.
  • Simulation on LTSPICE helped select components which will meet the system requirements.
PROJECT 02 — ANALOG SENSING

Analog Water Level Sensor

SYSTEM — Analog sensing & wireless transmission
METHOD — Simulation, bench testing, oscilloscope

Designed and built an analog sensing and transmission circuit to measure water levels, testing how different component choices affected range and accuracy.

  • Debugged and optimised the circuit so it would read accurately across 6 metres.
  • Used bench testing to compare real readings against expected behaviour and make targeted improvements to the design.
HANDS-ON TESTING
  • Tested the circuit using an oscilloscope to see which sub circuits can be optimised to increase transmission range and reduce signal loss over distance.
  • Using fundamentals we thought about which components effect range, the consequences of these changes and if they are worth it.
  • The project helped me understand how simulation results can vary from real life due losses, non ideal components and also noise.

PROJECT 03 — TEAM PROJECT

Smart Energy Monitor

MEASURES — Real-time voltage, current & power
PLATFORM — ESP32, 7-segment display

FIG. 3.1 — The sensing chain for voltage, current, comparison, and offset generation feeding the control system.

FIG. 3.2 — The finished monitor board displaying live voltage, current, and power readings.

Worked in a team to design and develop an energy sensor which displays voltage, current and power in real time.

  • Used bench instruments to check accuracy and traced issues back to their source by applying theory and using simulation to validate the fix.
  • Designed and tested the analog signal chain so the measured values remained accurate across the operating range.
BUILT & VERIFIED
  • The board displayed live readings on a 7-segment display, translating the design into a working measurement system.
  • Collaboration was important throughout the process, especially when identifying and fixing issues in the conditioning stages.
  • Measurement validation with both meters and an oscilloscope helped confirm the system was reliable rather than just theoretically correct.
